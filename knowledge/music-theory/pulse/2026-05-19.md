## 1. Scale Map

**E Phrygian Notes**: E F G A B C D

-   **Strongest Bass Foundation (Digitakt, Minibrute 2S)**:
    -   **E**: Root, absolute anchor.
    -   **A**: Perfect 4th, stable and heavy.
    -   **B**: Perfect 5th, powerful and grounding.
    -   **G**: Minor 3rd, solid dark foundation.
    -   *F (♭2)*: Use with care in the bass for intense tension; typically resolves to E.

-   **Safe Melody Notes on Subharmonicon Sequencer**:
    Given the Subharmonicon's strength in drones and polyrhythms, "safe" refers to notes that will create stable or intentionally dissonant intervals with the main E root drone. Its sequencers can be tuned.
    -   **VCO1 (Root)**: E (e.g., E2)
    -   **VCO2 (Interval)**: G (E to G is a minor 3rd), B (E to B is a perfect 5th), D (E to D is a minor 7th). These are harmonically stable.
    -   **Tension Note**: F (♭2). Integrating F on a Subharmonicon VCO2 or a sub-oscillator against an E drone creates immediate, sharp dissonance.

-   **Tension Note(s) for Maximum Dissonance against the Root (E)**:
    -   **F (♭2)**: The defining tension of Phrygian. It creates a half-step clash with the root, demanding resolution or holding for extreme grit.
    -   *B♭ (Diminished 5th)*: While not in E Phrygian, introducing a tritone against E would create maximum instability, suitable for specific chaotic moments.

-   **Recommended Lowest MIDI Note (Octave Placement) for Instruments**:
    -   **Digitakt (Bass Samples)**: E0 (MIDI note 28) for sub-bass, E1 (MIDI note 40) for punchy bass lines.
    -   **Minibrute 2S**: E1 (MIDI note 40) or E2 (MIDI note 52) for deep, monophonic lines.
    -   **Rev2**: E2 (MIDI note 52) for chord voicings, E3 (MIDI note 64) for leads and pads.
    -   **Take 5**: E2 (MIDI note 52) for thick chords, E3 (MIDI note 64) for punchy leads.
    -   **Pigments**: E2 (MIDI note 52) to E3 (MIDI note 64) – flexible, depending on patch design.
    -   **Subharmonicon**: VCO1 tuned to E2 (MIDI note 52) as the root reference.

## 2. Tension & Resolution Moves

Here are three specific harmonic tension/resolution techniques for E Phrygian:

1.  **The ♭II Cadence**:
    -   **Description**: The signature Phrygian move. A major chord built on the flat second degree creates intense tension against the E root, resolving back to the tonic.
    -   **Instruments**: Rev2 (pads/chords) and Minibrute 2S (bass).
    -   **Execution**:
        -   **Tension**: Minibrute 2S holds **E1** (MIDI note 40). Rev2 plays an **F major triad** voicing: **F2** (MIDI note 53), **A2** (MIDI note 57), **C3** (MIDI note 60). The clash between E bass and F major is immediate.
        -   **Resolution**: Minibrute 2S sustains **E1** (MIDI note 40). Rev2 drops back to an **E minor triad**: **E2** (MIDI note 52), **G2** (MIDI note 55), **B2** (MIDI note 59). The F major lifts, releasing into the stable minor.

2.  **Chromatic Ascent to Tension**:
    -   **Description**: A bass line that chromatically approaches the tension-defining F (♭2) from below, over a stable E drone, followed by a quick resolution.
    -   **Instruments**: Digitakt (sequencing bass via Minibrute 2S) and Pigments (pad).
    -   **Execution**:
        -   **Tension Build**: Pigments sustains a dark **E minor pad** (E2 G2 B2 D3). Digitakt sequences Minibrute 2S bass line: **D1** (MIDI note 38) → **E♭1** (MIDI note 39) → **E1** (MIDI note 40) → **F1** (MIDI note 41). The F1 arrival against the E pad is the peak tension.
        -   **Resolution**: The **F1** is held for 16th note, then immediately drops to a stable **E1** (MIDI note 40), resolving the chromaticism back to the root.

3.  **Dissonant Cluster to Open Power Chord**:
    -   **Description**: A dense, grinding cluster voicing incorporating the ♭2, used for a strong, abrasive hit, then opening up to a simpler, heavy power chord.
    -   **Instruments**: Take 5 (cluster), Digitakt (rhythmic accents).
    -   **Execution**:
        -   **Tension**: Take 5 plays a tight **E Phrygian cluster**: **E3** (MIDI note 64), **F3** (MIDI note 65), **G3** (MIDI note 67), **B3** (MIDI note 71). Digitakt hits a sharp, distorted percussion sample on the same beat.
        -   **Resolution**: Take 5 quickly releases to an **E5 power chord** (E root, B 5th): **E3** (MIDI note 64), **B3** (MIDI note 71). The density collapses into a stark, powerful interval.

## 3. Rhythm Grid (@ 108.0 BPM)

-   **Quarter note (1 beat)**: 556 ms
-   **8th note (½ beat)**: 278 ms
-   **16th note (¼ beat)**: 139 ms
-   **32nd note (⅛ beat)**: 70 ms (approx.)
-   **Dotted 8th (1.5 × 8th)**: 417 ms (common for syncopated snare hits, off-beat bass)
-   **Triplet 8th (⅓ × quarter)**: 185 ms (for fast, rolling figures)

-   **Digitakt Steps per Beat (16-step sequencer at 4/4)**:
    -   The Digitakt's 16-step sequencer, when set to a standard 4/4 bar, provides **4 steps per quarter note**. This means a 16th note is one step.

-   **Digitakt Step Length Values Alignment**:
    -   The Digitakt's internal step length (TRIG PAGE → LEN) and LFO speed settings (LFO PAGE → SPD) align directly with these timing values.
    -   Setting TRIG LEN to `1/16` will be 139 ms.
    -   LFO SPD set to `1/4` will cycle every 556 ms.
    -   These values are precise for locking rhythmic elements to the master tempo.

## 4. Chord Vocabulary (E Phrygian)

Here are 5 chords that work well in E Phrygian, with voicings and function:

1.  **Em (i)**
    -   **Notes**: E G B
    -   **Rev2 Voicing**: E2 (52), G2 (55), B2 (59)
    -   **Take 5 Voicing**: E2 (52), G2 (55), B2 (59)
    -   **Function**: Tonic — the home base, dark and stable.

2.  **Fmaj (♭II)**
    -   **Notes**: F A C
    -   **Rev2 Voicing**: F2 (53), A2 (57), C3 (60)
    -   **Take 5 Voicing**: F2 (53), A2 (57), C3 (60)
    -   **Function**: Subdominant with extreme tension — the defining Phrygian sound, often used as a pre-tonic chord.

3.  **Cmaj (♭VI)**
    -   **Notes**: C E G
    -   **Rev2 Voicing**: C3 (60), E3 (64), G3 (67)
    -   **Take 5 Voicing**: C3 (60), E3 (64), G3 (67)
    -   **Function**: Subdominant/Release — provides a strong, almost uplifting counterpoint to the minor tonality, a common minor chord progression.

4.  **E Phrygian Power Cluster (E F B)**
    -   **Notes**: E F B
    -   **Rev2 Voicing**: E2 (52), F2 (53), B2 (59) (A dense, dissonant power chord that emphasizes the ♭2)
    -   **Take 5 Voicing**: E2 (52), F2 (53), B2 (59)
    -   **Function**: Tonic with high dissonance – for heavy, grinding, impactful moments. The F (♭2) clashes with the E root, creating a powerful, unsettling bedrock.

5.  **Em(add♭9)**
    -   **Notes**: E F G B
    -   **Rev2 Voicing**: E2 (52), F2 (53), G2 (55), B2 (59)
    -   **Take 5 Voicing**: E2 (52), F2 (53), G2 (55), B2 (59) (This is 4 voices, ideal for Take 5)
    -   **Function**: Color/Tension — a dark, rich cluster that provides complex harmonic tension without needing immediate resolution, great for evolving pads or stabs.

## 5. Modulation Targets

Two keys/modes to pivot to for contrast sections:

1.  **Target Key/Mode**: E Aeolian (Natural Minor)
    -   **Pivot Note**: E (root), G (minor 3rd), B (perfect 5th), C (minor 6th), D (minor 7th). The main shift is the F (♭2) in Phrygian becoming F# (2nd) in Aeolian.
    -   **Execution**:
        -   **Transition**: Start with a sustained E minor chord or drone (Pigments). Over this, the Rev2 gradually introduces a melodic line that includes F# (MIDI note 54) instead of F (MIDI note 53), subtly hinting at the shift.
        -   **Chord Shift**: Replace instances of F major (♭II) with C major (♭VI) or D major (♭VII) for a more conventional minor feel. A rhythmic change on the Digitakt, perhaps a less frantic beat, can also signal the shift.
        -   **Emotional Shift**: From the aggressive, ancient, threatening feel of Phrygian to a more melancholic, dark, yet familiar and slightly more stable minor tonality. It offers a brief respite from the tension.

2.  **Target Key/Mode**: C Lydian
    -   **Pivot Note**: E (3rd of C), G (5th of C), B (7th of C), D (2nd of C). The key difference from E Phrygian is the F (♭2) becoming F# (♯4 of C Lydian). This is a dramatic shift.
    -   **Execution**:
        -   **Transition**: Begin with an abrupt stop or a dramatic sound effect. The Minibrute 2S can introduce a new, driving bass line on C. The Subharmonicon can tune its VCOs to C and G, creating a new drone.
        -   **Chord Shift**: Pigments introduces a bright, expansive pad in C Lydian (e.g., C3 D3 E3 F#3 G3 A3 B3). The absence of the Phrygian F will be immediately noticeable.
        -   **Emotional Shift**: A dramatic contrast from the heavy, internal tension of E Phrygian to an expansive, almost floating, and bright atmosphere. This could be used for an ethereal breakdown or a moment of unexpected, cinematic release before a re-entry into the Phrygian grit.

-   **Minibrute 2S LFO Rate Knob Positions**:
