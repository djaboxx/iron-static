## 1. Scale Map

**E F G A B C D**

*   **Strongest Bass Foundation (Digitakt bass track, Minibrute 2S):**
    *   **E** (Root): The anchor, always heavy.
    *   **A** (4th): Provides subdominant weight, common drone note.
    *   **B** (5th): Classic power, adds propulsion.
    *   **D** (♭7): Darker, pulls towards E.

*   **Safe Melody Notes on the Subharmonicon's Sequencer:**
    *   While all notes in the scale are 'safe', the Subharmonicon thrives on rhythmic and intervallic tension. Focus on **E, G, B** for foundation, and then introduce **F, C, D** for movement and darker melody. The ♭2 (F) can be used melodically to create sustained unease.

*   **Tension Note(s) against the Root (E):**
    *   **F (♭2):** The defining tension note of Phrygian. Maximize dissonance by holding F against E.
    *   **G (♭3):** While part of the minor tonic, it can create a 'grinding' minor 2nd against F when used in clusters.

*   **Recommended Lowest MIDI Note (Octave Placement) for Each Instrument:**
    *   **Digitakt (Bass Samples):** E0 – E1 (MIDI 24 – 36) for sub-bass impact.

    *   **Moog Subharmonicon:** Tuned VCOs typically operate higher. VCO1/VCO2 fundamental around C3 – C4 (MIDI 60 – 72) for drones, with subharmonics extending downwards. MIDI input focuses on triggering, not exact low note range.

## 2. Tension & Resolution Moves (E Phrygian)

Here are three specific harmonic tension/resolution techniques, actionable on the rig:

1.  **The ♭II Grind:**
    *   **Move:** Establish the E root with a heavy bass (Minibrute 2S or Digitakt bass sample). Layer an F major chord (♭II) on top, letting the F naturally clash with the E root.
    *   **Instruments & Notes:**
        *   **Minibrute 2S (or Digitakt Bass):** Sustained **E1** (MIDI 36).
        *   **Sequential Rev2 (Layer A):** F major voicing: **F2 A2 C3** (MIDI 53, 57, 60).
    *   **Resolution:** After 1-2 bars, drop the Rev2 chord to an E minor power chord.
        *   **Sequential Take 5:** E5 (power chord): **E2 B2** (MIDI 48, 59).
    *   **Sound:** A heavy, sustained, almost metallic grind, resolving to a foundational, albeit still dark, tonic.

2.  **The F-G Cluster Drop:**
    *   **Move:** Use a tight, dissonant cluster of the root (E), ♭2 (F), and ♭3 (G) for maximum aggression.
    *   **Instruments & Notes:**
        *   **Arturia Pigments:** A thick, detuned wavetable patch playing **E3 F3 G3** (MIDI 60, 61, 62). Use a fast attack and short decay for a percussive stab.
        *   **Digitakt:** Trigger a short, punchy sample (e.g., heavily distorted kick or clang) on the downbeat of this cluster.
    *   **Resolution:** Immediately after the cluster, drop the Pigments part to a single sustained **E2** (MIDI 48) on a long, slowly evolving pad sound.
    *   **Sound:** A sharp, jarring metallic hit, instantly giving way to a dark, deep drone that implies the root but retains tension from the previous hit.

3.  **♭II Melodic Pull:**
    *   **Move:** Introduce a simple, slow melodic line that emphasizes the F (♭2) over the E root, then pulls it down to E or G.
    *   **Instruments & Notes:**
        *   **Minibrute 2S (Sequencer Line):** A slowly rising and falling line: **E2 → F2 → G2 → F2 → E2** (MIDI 48, 50, 52, 50, 48) played slowly over 2-4 bars.
        *   **Sequential Rev2 (Pad):** A sustained E minor 7th voicing: **E2 G2 B2 D3** (MIDI 48, 52, 55, 59).
    *   **Resolution:** The melodic line naturally resolves back to the root or minor 3rd, creating a brief sense of release within the overall darkness. The Rev2 pad provides a consistent, heavy backdrop.
    *   **Sound:** A mournful, almost vocal lead line that twists around the root, building and releasing small pockets of melodic unease.

    *   A 16-step pattern (the Digitakt's default resolution) means 4 steps per quarter note in 4/4 time.

*   **Digitakt Step Length Values Alignment (relative to a quarter note pulse):**
    *   **1/8:** An 8th note duration. This means the sound will last for 2 Digitakt steps if the pattern is 16 steps per 4 beats.
    *   **1/16:** A 16th note duration. The sound will last for 1 Digitakt step.
    *   **1/32:** A 32nd note duration. This is shorter than a single Digitakt step, often used for very short, clicky sounds or quickly gated elements.

For example, a "dotted 8th" feel can be created by placing a note on a 16th step and then adjusting its micro-timing forward by one 32nd note, or by using track-level tempo multipliers.

## 4. Chord Vocabulary (E Phrygian)

Here are 5 chords for E Phrygian, with voicing suggestions for the rig:

1.  **Em (E minor) — Tonic Chord**
    *   **Notes:** E G B
    *   **Rev2 Voicing:** E2 G2 B2 (MIDI 48, 52, 55) — solid, dark foundation.
    *   **Take 5 Voicing:** E3 G3 B3 (MIDI 60, 64, 67) — punchy, less sustain.
    *   **Function:** Stable tonic. Can be sustained as a pad or played percussively.

2.  **Fmaj (F major) — ♭II Chord**
    *   **Notes:** F A C
    *   **Rev2 Voicing:** F2 A2 C3 (MIDI 53, 57, 60) — defining Phrygian sound, strong tension against E.
    *   **Take 5 Voicing:** F3 A3 C4 (MIDI 65, 69, 72) — brighter, cutting version of the tension chord.
    *   **Function:** Subdominant, creates intense dissonance against the E root, pulling back to Em.

3.  **Dm (D minor) — ♭VII Chord**
    *   **Notes:** D F A
    *   **Rev2 Voicing:** D2 F2 A2 (MIDI 50, 53, 57) — dark, melancholic.
    *   **Take 5 Voicing:** D3 F3 A3 (MIDI 62, 65, 69) — can be used for rhythmic stabs.
    *   **Function:** Subdominant, provides a familiar minor cadence, often leading to Em or Fmaj.

4.  **E-Cluster (E-F-G) — Dissonant Tonic Cluster**
    *   **Notes:** E F G
    *   **Rev2 Voicing:** E2 F2 G2 (MIDI 48, 53, 55) — use a very short release envelope for a percussive, jarring stab.
    *   **Take 5 Voicing:** E3 F3 G3 (MIDI 60, 61, 62) — can be even punchier, or use a longer release with the filter almost closed for a growl.
    *   **Function:** Extreme tension, harmonic impact, used for chaotic or abrasive moments.

5.  **E5 (E Power Chord equivalent) — Foundational Weight**
    *   **Notes:** E B (omitting the 3rd, ambiguous minor/major but heavy)
    *   **Minibrute 2S & Digitakt (layering):** Minibrute 2S plays **E2** (MIDI 48) while Digitakt samples play **B1** (MIDI 47) for a deep, rumbling fifth.
    *   **Rev2/Take 5 Voicing:** E2 B2 (MIDI 48, 55) — simple, raw power. Use a thick detuned patch.
    *   **Function:** Core rhythmic and harmonic anchor, maximum weight and drive without overt melodic content.

## 5. Modulation Targets

Two keys/modes to pivot to for contrast sections from E Phrygian:

1.  **Target: E Natural Minor (Aeolian)**
    *   **Pivot Note:** E (shared root). The notes E, G, A, B, C, D are common.
    *   **Execution:** The key change is subtle, from E Phrygian (E F G A B C D) to E Natural Minor (E F# G A B C D). The defining move is raising the **F (♭2)** to **F# (2nd)**.
        *   **Minibrute 2S/Digitakt (bass):** Stays on E.
        *   **Sequential Rev2 (pad/chords):** Transition from F major chords to C major or G major chords (which are diatonic to E minor). For instance, an Fmaj chord (F A C) moves to a Cmaj chord (C E G) over the E bass.
        *   **Arturia Pigments (lead):** Introduce an F# into lead lines, avoiding F.
    *   **Emotional Shift:** From tense, aggressive, and ancient-feeling to more familiar, melancholic, and perhaps a touch more driving. It offers a slight release of the Phrygian tension, making the return to Phrygian even more impactful.

2.  **Target: A Phrygian Dominant**
    *   **Pivot Note:** A (shared 4th of E Phrygian, becomes root of A Phrygian Dominant). Notes D, E, F are shared.
    *   **Execution:** Shift the tonal center to A, and introduce the distinctive G# of A Phrygian Dominant (A ♭B C# D E F G). From E Phrygian (E F G A B C D), the key changes from E to A, and the G becomes G#.
        *   **Digitakt/Minibrute 2S (bass):** Lead the change by shifting the prominent bass note from E to A.
        *   **Sequential Take 5 (stabs/chords):** Introduce A Phrygian Dominant voicings. A power chord (A-E) as the new tonic, then incorporate a C# and G# into subsequent chords. For example, **Am(maj3)**: A C# E G.
        *   **Arturia Pigments (evolving texture):** Use a slow, detuned sweep that emphasizes the new G# and C#, creating an unsettling, exotic backdrop.
    *   **Emotional Shift:** A dramatic shift from dark, aggressive Phrygian to an even more exotic, dangerous, and almost hypnotic feel. This creates a strong contrast section, like entering a completely different sonic landscape.
