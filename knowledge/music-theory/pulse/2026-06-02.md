## 2. Tension & Resolution Moves

Here are three specific harmonic tension/resolution techniques for E Phrygian at 108 BPM:

1.  **The "Crushing ♭II" Cadence**:

    *   **Resolution**: Rev2 drops from F major to an E minor power chord: E3 (MIDI 52) and B3 (MIDI 59), while the Minibrute 2S sustains E. The F major's dissonant pull resolves back to the stable, heavy minor tonic.
2.  **The "Chromatic Grind" Lead**:
    *   **Move**: A short, aggressive lead line using the ♭2 (F) as a pivot, creating chromatic friction.
    *   **Instruments**:

    *   **Resolution**: The Pigments lead immediately descends back to A3 (MIDI 57) or G3 (MIDI 55), pulling back to a "safer" minor scale tone. The DFAM hit subsides.
3.  **The "Suspended B♭" Drop**:
    *   **Move**: Leverage the ♭6 (C) and ♭7 (D) of E Phrygian to build a suspended tension over the B (5th) chord. This is often achieved with a Gsus4, which effectively contains C and D.

        *   **Take 5**: A dissonant interval of B3 (MIDI 59) and F4 (MIDI 65) playing stabs over the Gsus4.
    *   **Resolution**: Rev2 resolves the Gsus4 to a G major chord (G3, B3, D4) (MIDI 55, 59, 62), followed by a return to E minor (Em power chord E3, B3 - MIDI 52, 59) on Take 5. The suspension releases into a more consonant minor harmony.
