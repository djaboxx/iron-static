## 2. Tension & Resolution Moves

Here are three specific harmonic tension/resolution techniques for E Phrygian:

1.  **The ♭II Grind (F Major over E Bass)**:
    *   **Description**: This is the signature Phrygian cadence. The F major chord creates immediate, unsettling tension against the E root, conveying an oppressive and heavy feel.
    *   **Instruments**:
        *   **Minibrute 2S**: Steady E1 (28) bass drone or rhythmic pulse.
        *   **Sequential Rev2**: Plays an F major chord voicing: F2 (41), A2 (45), C3 (48).
    *   **Resolution**: Drop the Rev2 voicing back to an E minor chord (E2 G2 B2) or a D minor chord (D2 F2 A2), while the Minibrute 2S sustains the E bass. The tension of the F major "grinds" into the relative stability of the minor.

2.  **Chromatic Ascent to the ♭2 (F)**:
    *   **Description**: Build micro-tension by approaching the crucial ♭2 (F) chromatically from below, creating a jarring, industrial "siren" effect.
    *   **Instruments**:
        *   **Moog DFAM**: Provides a percussive, off-kilter rhythmic bed.
        *   **Arturia Pigments**: Plays a short, sharp lead melody.
    *   **Execution**:
        *   **Pigments**: Play E3 (52) for 100ms, then E♭3 (51) for 100ms, then E3 (52) for 100ms, finally landing on F3 (53) for 250ms. Repeat.
    *   **Resolution**: The F3 itself is the tension point. Resolution occurs by moving the Pigments line to G3 (55) or D3 (50), providing a temporary melodic release within the scale, or dropping the bassline from E to A on the Minibrute 2S, shifting harmonic focus.

3.  **The ♭6 Drone Swell (C over G)**:
    *   **Description**: Use the ♭6 (C) as a sustained, melancholic drone over a moving bass or pedal tone, building atmospheric tension that eventually collapses.
    *   **Instruments**:
        *   **Sequential Take 5**: Sustained C3 (48) with a slow attack and long release.
        *   **Elektron Digitakt**: Sequences a low G1 (31) bassline, moving to D1 (26).
    *   **Execution**:
        *   **Take 5**: Holds C3 (48) for 4 bars, slowly opening the filter (using CC74).
        *   **Digitakt**: Plays a G1 (31) on beats 1 and 3 for 2 bars, then D1 (26) on beats 1 and 3 for 2 bars, then repeats.
    *   **Resolution**: The Take 5 drops out or fades into silence, leaving the Digitakt's G-D bassline to resolve to an E bassline on the Minibrute 2S. The sustained C over G creates a longing tension, resolved by its removal.

**Digitakt Step Alignment (16-step sequencer at 4/4):**

-   A 16-step pattern represents 4 quarter notes.
-   **1 Quarter Note = 4 Digitakt steps** (e.g., placing a trig on step 1, 5, 9, 13)
-   **1 8th Note = 2 Digitakt steps** (e.g., trigs on step 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15)
-   **1 16th Note = 1 Digitakt step** (e.g., trigs on every step from 1 to 16)
-   **1 32nd Note**: Requires micro-timing on Digitakt, shifting a single 16th step by `+6` or `-6` (out of `+12/-12` total per step) from its central position. This means two 32nd notes can fit within a single 16th step.
-   **Dotted 8th**: Achieved by placing a trig on a 16th step, and setting its `LEN` (note length) parameter to `3/16` or triggering an additional silent trig 3 steps later on a new track.
-   **Triplet 8th**: Use the `TRP` (Triplet) setting on the Digitakt sequencer scale, which divides a step into triplets, allowing three notes within the space of two 16th notes (or one 8th note).

## 4. Chord Vocabulary

Five chords that work well in E Phrygian, with voicings for polyphonic synths:

1.  **E minor triad (Em)**
    *   **Notes**: E G B
    *   **Rev2 Voicing**: E2 (40), G2 (43), B2 (47)
    *   **Take 5 Voicing**: E3 (52), G3 (55), B3 (59)
    *   **Function**: Tonic — the home base, but with underlying tension due to the Phrygian context. Power-chord equivalent: E1 (28), B1 (35), E2 (40) for Minibrute 2S or Digitakt bass.

2.  **F Major triad (F)**
    *   **Notes**: F A C
    *   **Rev2 Voicing**: F2 (41), A2 (45), C3 (48)
    *   **Take 5 Voicing**: F3 (53), A3 (57), C4 (60)
    *   **Function**: ♭II chord — The defining Phrygian tension chord. Use this for aggressive harmonic pushes.

3.  **G Major triad (G)**
    *   **Notes**: G B D
    *   **Rev2 Voicing**: G2 (43), B2 (47), D3 (50)
    *   **Take 5 Voicing**: G3 (55), B3 (59), D4 (62)
    *   **Function**: ♭III chord — Provides a momentary lift or a slightly more traditional minor feel when used in progression, acting as a relative major.

4.  **D minor triad (Dm)**
    *   **Notes**: D F A
    *   **Rev2 Voicing**: D2 (50), F2 (41), A2 (45) (inverted for lower register)
    *   **Take 5 Voicing**: D3 (62), F3 (53), A3 (57)
    *   **Function**: ♭VII chord — Offers a strong root movement back to the E minor tonic, or can be used as a subdominant function.

5.  **E-F-G Cluster (E2-F2-G2)**
    *   **Notes**: E F G (a tight, dissonant minor second and major second stack)
    *   **Rev2 Voicing**: E2 (40), F2 (41), G2 (43) — use a very slow attack and high resonance on the Curtis filter for maximum ominousness.
    *   **Take 5 Voicing**: E3 (52), F3 (53), G3 (55) — use for punchy, short stabs or heavily distorted textures.
    *   **Function**: Color/Tension — Not a functional triad, but a dense, abrasive cluster that perfectly embodies the "heavy, weird" aesthetic. Ideal for sustained, low drones or short, violent stabs.

## 5. Modulation Targets

Two keys/modes for effective contrast sections from E Phrygian:

1.  **Target Key/Mode: E Natural Minor (Aeolian)**
    *   **Pivot Notes Shared with E Phrygian**: E, G, A, B, C, D. The key difference is the 2nd degree: F in Phrygian becomes F# in Aeolian.
    *   **Execution**:
        *   **Transition Sound**: Shift from the harsh ♭2 (F) of Phrygian to the more traditional 2nd (F#) of Aeolian. This subtly "opens up" the sound without losing the minor feel.
        *   **Instrument Lead**: The **Sequential Rev2** could lead the modulation by introducing melodic phrases or chord voicings that incorporate F# (e.g., D major triad over E bass, or a G major 7 chord emphasizing F#). The **Minibrute 2S** could maintain the E bass drone to ground the transition.
        *   **Emotional Shift**: From "threatening" or "oppressive" to "melancholic" or "darkly determined." It retains heaviness but becomes less immediately confrontational, offering a moment of slightly more familiar minor-key darkness before potentially returning to the Phrygian tension.

2.  **Target Key/Mode: B Locrian**
    *   **Pivot Notes Shared with E Phrygian**: B, C, D, E, F, G, A. E Phrygian has E as root; B Locrian has B as root. Many shared notes, but the tonal center shifts. The crucial difference is the ♭5 of Locrian (F) and the diminished tonic triad of B Locrian (B D F).
    *   **Execution**:
        *   **Transition Sound**: A sudden, disorienting shift where the sense of tonal center collapses. The F (♭2 in E Phrygian) becomes the ♭5 in B Locrian, creating extreme instability around the new root B.
        *   **Instrument Lead**: The **Moog Subharmonicon** could pivot to a B drone, with its subharmonic oscillators tuned to intervals that emphasize the diminished quality (e.g., B, D, F). The **Arturia Pigments** could play chaotic, atonal arpeggios using the B Locrian scale (B C D E F G A), focusing on clusters that highlight the diminished triad B-D-F. The **DFAM** could shift to an even more erratic, non-quantized rhythmic pattern.
        *   **Emotional Shift**: From "tense" to "unhinged," "chaotic," or "structurally collapsing." This would be ideal for a breakdown, an abstract industrial interlude, or a section of maximum disorientation before a forceful return to the E Phrygian groove.

**Digitakt LFO Multiplier Settings**: The Digitakt's LFO `SYNC` mode automatically ties the LFO rate to tempo divisions (e.g., `1/1`, `1/2`, `1/4`, `1/8`, `1/16`, `1/32`). The `SPD` parameter can be used as a multiplier when `SYNC` is off, or to fine-tune rates around synchronous values. **Minibrute 2S LFO Rate Knob Positions**: The Minibrute 2S LFO `RATE` knob generally covers a range from sub-audio (very slow, below 1 Hz) to audio rates (above 20 Hz).
-   Values below 1 Hz (1 bar, Half note, Dotted quarter) will typically be found in the **low range** (fully counter-clockwise to about 10 o'clock).
-   Values between 1 Hz and 5 Hz (Quarter note, 8th note, Triplet 8th) will be in the **mid-range** (roughly 11 o'clock to 2 o'clock). Fine-tuning will be necessary to hit exact values. Use an external LFO measurement tool if precise sync is critical and MIDI sync is not an option.
