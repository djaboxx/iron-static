## Elektron Digitakt MK1

Here are two preset ideas for the Elektron Digitakt MK1, tailored for the active song "Ignition Point."

### Preset 1: Forge Hammer Kit — Rusted, relentless, hydraulic, punishing groove

**Concept:** This is the core rhythmic engine for "Ignition Point," blending industrial weight with a driving metal groove. It's designed to be the solid, unyielding foundation that the rest of the track pushes against.

**Synthesis Approach:**
- **Engine / oscillator setup:** This is a sample-based kit. Kick: Saturated 909 or a sample of a heavy door slam, pitched down. Snare: A layered sample of a punchy acoustic snare and a metallic pipe impact. Hats: Industrial hiss or a sample of pressurized air being released, with a tight decay.
- **Filter starting point:** Global filter is mostly open. Use the track's Low Pass filter on the kick to control the click (Cutoff ~75). On the snare, a slight High Pass filter (Cutoff ~20) to clear out low-end mud.
- **Envelope character:** Kick/Snare have an instant attack and a controlled decay to maintain maximum punch. Hats have a very fast attack and a very short decay (~40) to keep them tight and percussive.
- **Modulation:** LFO1 assigned to the Sample Start point on the hi-hat track, with a very small depth and a "random" waveform to create subtle, humanizing variations that prevent the loop from feeling static.
- **Effects:** A medium plate reverb send on the snare, used sparingly. A dotted-eighth note delay send on a single, parameter-locked ghost note or fill hit.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
- `[SRC] PITCH` (Kick Track): -18
- `[FILTER] FREQ` (Kick Track): 75
- `[AMP] HOLD` (Hat Track): 0
- `[AMP] DEC` (Hat Track): 40
- `[LFO1] DEST` (Hat Track): Sample Start
- `[LFO1] DEPTH` (Hat Track): 5-8
- `[FX] REV SEND` (Snare Track): 30 (parameter-locked on specific trigs)
- `[FX] DEL SEND` (Snare Track): 45 (parameter-locked on fill trigs)

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
- **When to reach for this sound:** This is the primary A/B section beat for verses and choruses. Build energy by adding/removing the hat or snare layers, or by increasing the reverb sends in a pre-chorus.
- **Digitakt integration hint:** Use the Digitakt's MIDI tracks to sequence a bassline that locks into this kit's groove. Automate CC74 (Filter Frequency) on a dedicated percussion track (e.g., a noisy tom) during a fill for a classic sweep.
- **One thing that makes it heavy or weird:** The heaviness comes from the sample choice and punchy envelopes. The weirdness is in the LFO-driven sample start modulation, making the hi-hats feel like a slightly malfunctioning, jittery machine.

### Preset 2: Shrapnel Swarm — Digital rust, chaotic, chattering, broken

**Concept:** A textural percussion layer designed to create rhythmic tension and an atmosphere of digital decay. This isn't for driving the beat, but for making it feel alive, unstable, and dangerous.

**Synthesis Approach:**
- **Engine / oscillator setup:** Load a single, long textural sample (e.g., radio static, granular noise, a field recording of a failing hard drive) onto a track. The "hits" will be sculpted by triggering tiny slices of this evolving sound.
- **Filter starting point:** A resonant Band Pass filter (BP) is crucial. Use it to isolate interesting, shifting frequencies in the noise sample, turning texture into tonal blips.
- **Envelope character:** Extremely fast attack and a very short decay/hold time. The goal is to create percussive "clicks," "pops," and "zaps" from the source sample, not let it drone.
- **Modulation:** LFO1 is key. Set it to a random or noise shape and have it aggressively modulate the Filter Cutoff frequency. This makes the swarm "chatter" as different harmonics are excited with each trig.
- **Effects:** Add significant Bit Reduction to introduce digital grit and aliasing. Use a long, dark reverb to wash the short stabs out into a ghostly, atmospheric tail.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
- `[SRC] START`: Use parameter locks to set this to a different value on each trig.
- `[FILTER] TYPE`: BP
- `[FILTER] FREQ`: 64 (this will be heavily modulated)
- `[FILTER] RES`: 100
- `[AMP] DEC`: 15 (very short)
- `[LFO1] DEST`: Filter Frequency
- `[LFO1] DEPTH`: 60
- `[EFFECTS] BIT REDUCTION`: 25

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
- **When to reach for this sound:** Use during intros, breakdowns, or as a subtle high-frequency noise layer during a heavy chorus to add a sense of chaos. It's the opposite of a clean shaker.
- **Digitakt integration hint:** Program a very sparse pattern using conditional trigs (e.g., 25%, 2:4) so the swarm appears semi-randomly. Use parameter locks on CC10 (Pan) to have these hits jump around the stereo field.
- **One thing that makes it heavy or weird:** The weirdness is the core concept: creating a rhythmic part from a non-percussive, noisy sample. The heaviness it adds is textural; it makes the whole mix feel more abrasive and unstable, in the spirit of Nine Inch Nails' more chaotic moments.

## Sequential Take 5

### Preset 1: Phrygian Glitch — Dissonant, percussive, rhythmic stabs

**Concept:** This is a harsh, metallic chord stab designed for rhythmic punctuation rather than melodic harmony. It uses a fast, tempo-synced LFO to create a glitching, stuttering effect that can accent the main drum groove.

**Synthesis Approach:**
- Engine / oscillator setup: Two saw oscillators. OSC 2 is hard-synced to OSC 1. Set OSC 2's frequency about a perfect fifth above OSC 1 to create harsh, metallic overtones when its pitch is swept.
- Filter starting point: 4-pole Low Pass filter. Cutoff set low (~30%), resonance high (~75%) to create a sharp, resonant "zap."
- Envelope character: Very fast attack (<5ms), quick decay (~150ms), zero sustain, and a short release (~50ms). This creates a tight, percussive stab.
- Modulation: A fast, tempo-synced Sample & Hold LFO is routed to OSC 2's pitch. This creates the signature rhythmic glitching and harmonic chaos.
- Effects: A touch of distortion to add grit, and a simple, dark delay mixed low.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
- `Filter Cutoff`: 35%
- `Filter Resonance`: 80%
- `Filter Env Amount`: +70
- `LFO1 Freq`: 1/16 (synced to BPM)
- `LFO1 Shape`: Sample & Hold
- `LFO1 Amount` (to OSC 2 Freq): 45
- `OSC 2 Hard Sync`: On
- `FX Depth/Mix`: 20% (Delay)

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
- When to reach for this sound: Use it in place of a ride cymbal or hi-hat to create a driving, tense rhythmic bed, or for syncopated accents during a heavy breakdown.
- Digitakt integration hint: Sequence on MIDI track 4 (ch4). Use parameter locks on `LFO1 Freq` (CC75) to switch between 1/16 and 1/8th note glitches for rhythmic variation. P-lock `Filter Cutoff` (CC33) on specific steps to make them scream.
- One thing that makes it heavy or weird: The hard-synced oscillator being modulated by a Sample & Hold LFO creates a controlled chaos that feels like a machine tearing itself apart in perfect time.

-   VCF RESONANCE: 50%

### Preset 2: Phrygian Glitch — Rhythmic, chaotic, percussive, frantic, digital

**Concept:** A chaotic, sequenced lead that spits out semi-random patterns locked to the E Phrygian scale. This is for adding fast, unpredictable electronic texture and ear candy.

**Synthesis Approach:**
-   **Engine / oscillator setup**:
    -   Engine 1: Sample engine in Granular mode, loaded with a simple vocal "ah" or metallic hit.
    -   Engine 2: Analog engine, simple Saw wave, tuned -12 semitones for sub-octave support.
-   **Filter starting point**: Filter 1 is an aggressive MS-20 band-pass filter, Cutoff at ~1.5 kHz, Resonance cranked to 75% to create a piercing, tonal bite.
-   **Envelope character**: Amp Env: instant attack, ~150ms decay, zero sustain. Filter Env: even shorter, a 50ms decay mapped to F1 Cutoff to create a sharp "pluck."
-   **Modulation**:
    -   Pigments' internal Sequencer/Arp is the star. Set to Arpeggiator mode, 16th notes, 2 octaves. Use the randomize/probability functions on notes and gates.
    -   Macro 1 (CC20) controls Grain Density. Macro 2 (CC21) controls F1 Cutoff (CC74).
-   **Effects**: Bit Crusher to add digital artifacts, followed by a fast, ping-pong Delay synced to 16th notes.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
-   Arp/Seq > Rate: 1/16
-   Arp/Seq > Gate Length: ~20%
-   Arp/Seq > Note Probability: ~70% (to create gaps)
-   Engine 1 (Granular) > Density: 50 Hz
-   Engine 1 (Granular) > Grain Size: 40 ms
-   Filter 1 > Cutoff (CC74): 1.5 kHz
-   Filter 1 > Resonance (CC71): 0.75
-   Macro 1 (CC20): Controls Grain Density (+30 Hz) and Grain Size (-20 ms)

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
-   **When to reach for this sound**: As a counter-rhythm to a heavy drum groove or as a chaotic layer during a transition or climax. Hold down a simple E minor chord and let the arp do the work.
-   **Digitakt integration hint**: Program a simple chord on MIDI channel 8. Use parameter locks on the Digitakt's MIDI track to automate CC20 and CC21, creating rhythmic shifts in the glitch texture and filter sweeps.
-   **One thing that makes it heavy or weird**: The probabilistic arpeggiator combined with the granular engine ensures the pattern never repeats exactly, creating a sense of controlled chaos that feels both machine-driven and alive.
