## Elektron Digitakt MK1

Here are two preset ideas for the Elektron Digitakt MK1, tailored for the active song "Ignition Point."

### Preset 1: Corroded Clockwork — Grimy, rhythmic, foundational industrial pulse

**Concept:** This preset turns a non-musical sample of grinding metal into the rhythmic heart of a track. It’s not a drum, but a textural engine that provides a sense of mechanical decay and relentless momentum, sitting underneath the main drums.

**Synthesis Approach:**
-   **Engine / oscillator setup**: Load a 2-4 second mono sample of metal scraping, a failing machine, or a factory floor. The specific source isn't as important as the noisy, harmonically rich texture.
-   **Filter starting point**: A low-pass filter (LPF) set to be dark and resonant, creating a focused, thudding sound from the noisy source. Cutoff: 55, Resonance: 68.
-   **Envelope character**: Plucky and tight. Instant attack, ~60ms decay, no sustain, minimal release. This carves percussive hits out of the continuous sample noise.
-   **Modulation**: LFO1 is key. Set its mode to "Random" or "Sample and Hold," targeting the Sample Start parameter (`STA`) with a moderate depth. This ensures each triggered hit plays a different slice of the sample, avoiding sterile repetition.
-   **Effects**: Heavy send to the internal Delay with high feedback and filtering, creating a washed-out, dubby trail. Minimal Reverb send, just enough to give it a sense of space.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
1.  `SAMPLE`: A mono .wav file of industrial field recording.
2.  `TRIG`: Set a simple 4/4 pattern on steps 1, 5, 9, 13 to establish the pulse.
3.  `SRC: STA`: 20 (The LFO will move this around).
4.  `FILTER: FREQ` (CC74): 55
5.  `FILTER: RES`: 68
6.  `LFO1: DEST`: `SRC:STA` (Sample Start)
7.  `LFO1: SPD`: 32
8.  `FX: DELAY SEND` (CC82): 80

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
-   **When to reach for this sound**: Use it as the foundational rhythmic layer in an intro or verse. Let it run constantly while other drums punch in and out, creating a "machine-womb" atmosphere.
-   **Digitakt integration hint**: Sequence this on Audio Track 8. Use parameter locks to drastically open the Filter Frequency (CC74) on the last beat of a 4-bar phrase for a dramatic fill.
-   **One thing that makes it heavy or weird**: The LFO modulating the sample start means the texture is constantly shifting. It feels alive and unpredictable, like a machine that might break down at any moment.

### Preset 2: Stochastic Swarm — Chaotic, unpredictable, sequenced monoline

**Concept:** This preset is not a sound, but a MIDI sequencing pattern designed to drive an external monosynth. It uses conditional trigs and parameter-locked MIDI CCs to generate a frantic, semi-melodic burst of notes that sounds like a swarm of angry hornets, perfect for adding high-frequency chaos.

**Synthesis Approach:**
-   **Engine / oscillator setup**: N/A (This is a MIDI track controlling another synth, like the Minibrute 2S or Pigments).
-   **Note Grid**: A 16-step sequence populated with 10-12 notes from the E Phrygian scale (e.g., E, F, A, B). Place them in a seemingly random, dense pattern.
-   **Conditional Trigs**: The core of the idea. Set most of the trigs to have a low probability (e.g., 33% or 50%). Set a few key trigs to `1:4` (only plays the first time through a 4-bar loop), creating moments of stability before returning to chaos.
-   **Modulation (via P-Locks)**: Parameter-lock MIDI CCs on specific steps. On 3-4 random steps, lock the synth's filter cutoff (CC74) to a high value (e.g., 120). On 2-3 other steps, lock the LFO depth (CC109) to a high value.
-   **Microtiming**: Nudge several trigs slightly forward or backward in time to break the rigidity of the grid and enhance the frantic, rushing feel.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
1.  `TRACK`: A MIDI Track (e.g., MIDI Track 7).
2.  `SRC: CHAN`: 7 (To target the Arturia Minibrute 2S, per the default map).
3.  `TRIG`: Populate 12 of the 16 steps with notes.
4.  `TRIG: TRC (Condition)`: Set 8 of the 12 trigs to a probability of `40%`.
5.  `P-LOCK 1`: Lock `CC 74` to `120` on steps 3, 7, and 14.
6.  `P-LOCK 2`: Lock `CC 109` to `100` on steps 5 and 11.
7.  `P-LOCK 3`: Lock `VEL` (Velocity) to `127` on all p-locked steps for extra accent.
8.  `NOTE: MTIM`: Nudge 4-5 trigs off-grid by +/- `1/384`.

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
-   **When to reach for this sound**: Deploy this during a high-energy chorus or a chaotic breakdown section. It's too intense for a verse but perfect for adding a layer of controlled madness over a heavy groove.
-   **Digitakt integration hint**: Route this MIDI track to your most aggressive monosynth. Keep the pattern muted, then unmute it for 4-8 bars at a time as a dramatic effect. Tweak the assigned CCs in real-time for performance.
-   **One thing that makes it heavy or weird**: Its unpredictability. Because of the conditional trigs, the sequence never plays the same way twice, generating a new frantic melody with each pass. It embodies the "inevitability from chaos" principle.

## Sequential Rev2

### Preset 1: Phrygian Rust — Dissonant, detuned, unstable, tense

**Concept:** This is a pad that leans into the inherent tension of the E Phrygian scale. It uses slight detuning and slow, asynchronous modulation to create a sense of mechanical decay and harmonic instability, perfect for a brooding intro or a tense bridge.

**Synthesis Approach:**
-   **Engine / oscillator setup:** Both oscillators on sawtooth waves. OSC 2 is tuned down a perfect fifth (-7 semitones) and slightly detuned (-8 cents) from OSC 1 to create a heavy, dissonant foundation. Add a small amount of oscillator slop to introduce analog drift.
-   **Filter starting point:** 4-pole Low Pass filter with cutoff around 45% and resonance at 30%. Key tracking should be at 50% to open the filter slightly for higher notes.
-   **Envelope character:** Amp Env: slow attack (~1.5s), long decay (~4s), high sustain (~80%), and a medium release (~2s). Filter Env: similar but with a slightly faster attack to create a gentle swell.
-   **Modulation:** LFO 1 (triangle wave, slow rate ~0.2 Hz) is routed to OSC 1 fine tune with a very small depth to create a subtle, warbling pitch instability. LFO 2 (random/S&H, faster rate ~3 Hz) is routed to Pan with a small amount to create a skittering stereo image.
-   **Effects:** Use the onboard reverb with a medium decay and a high mix to wash the sound out.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
-   `OSC 1 Freq`: E3
-   `OSC 2 Freq`: A2
-   `OSC 2 Fine`: -8
-   `Oscillator Slop`: 25
-   `Filter Cutoff`: 55
-   `Filter Resonance`: 40
-   `Amp Env Attack`: 100
-   `LFO 1 Freq`: 15

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
-   **When to reach for this sound:** Use it to establish a tense atmosphere under a simple drum pattern or as a textural bed during a breakdown before a heavy drop.
-   **Digitakt integration hint:** Sequence long, sustained chords from the Digitakt on MIDI channel 2. Automate `Filter Cutoff` (CC 105) with slow LFOs from the Digitakt to create evolving filter sweeps that are out of sync with the patch's internal LFOs.
-   **One thing that makes it heavy or weird:** The fifth-down detuned second oscillator creates a constant, unresolved harmonic cluster that makes even simple root-fifth chords sound menacing and full.

-   **Filter starting point:** 4-pole Low Pass filter with a low cutoff (~20%) and high resonance (~70%) to create a sharp, almost self-oscillating point.

-   **Effects:** Use the onboard distortion effect to add grit and harmonic saturation. Keep it subtle to avoid turning the sound to mush.

**Parameter Starting Points:**

-   `OSC 2 Sync`: On
-   `Filter Cutoff`: 30
-   `Filter Resonance`: 85
-   `Filter Env Amount`: 90
-   `Filter Env Decay`: 45

-   `Distortion Amount`: 20

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
-   **When to reach for this sound:** As a primary riff or a counter-melody during a high-energy verse or chorus. It can carry the melodic weight while the bass and drums lock down the groove.
-   **Digitakt integration hint:** Send a single, sustained note from a Digitakt MIDI track on channel 2 to trigger the Rev2's gated sequencer. Use parameter locks on the Digitakt to change the note, effectively transposing the sequence on the fly. Automate `Distortion Amount` (CC 15) to ramp up intensity.

### Preset 2: Broken Carrier — Unstable, metallic, atonal, evolving

**Concept:** A monophonic lead sound that feels like a damaged transmission or a failing machine. It uses audio-rate modulation to generate inharmonic sidebands, creating a sound that's more noise and texture than melody.

**Synthesis Approach:**
-   **Engine / oscillator setup:** Both VCOs set to Sine waves. Oscillator Sync engaged. This clean signal will be the "carrier" for the modulation.
-   **Filter starting point:** Filter fully open (Cutoff 100%), Resonance at 10%. The character comes from the modulation, not from filtering.
-   **Envelope character:** Simple gate envelope: instant attack, full sustain, ~150ms release.
-   **Modulation:** The core of the sound. LFO1 is set to a Sawtooth wave, cranked into audio rate, and routed to modulate the pitch of VCO2. This creates harsh, metallic FM-like tones.
-   **Effects:** A BBD-style delay with high feedback, synced to the tempo, to smear the harsh tones into a rhythmic, decaying wash.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
-   LFO1 Freq (CC 75): 50 Hz (audio-rate)
-   LFO1 Amount (CC 76) -> VCO 2 Pitch: 127 (max)
-   LFO1 Wave: Saw
-   Oscillator Sync: On
-   Filter Cutoff (CC 33): 1023 (max)
-   Filter Resonance (CC 34): ~15
-   FX Depth/Mix (CC 18): 70
-   FX Time/Rate: Dotted 8th

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
-   **When to reach for this sound:** As a textural element in an intro or breakdown, or as a high-frequency noise layer. Hold a single note and let its evolving texture create a sense of unease.
-   **Digitakt integration hint:** On MIDI track 4, automate the LFO1 Freq (CC 75) with the LFO designer or parameter locks. This will rhythmically shift the timbre of the metallic noise, turning it into a chaotic lead.
-   **One thing that makes it heavy or weird:** Using an LFO at audio rate to modulate a synced oscillator completely deconstructs the traditional synth voice, turning it into a harsh, industrial noise generator that is still technically "in key" but sounds anything but.

### Preset 1: Unstable Clockwork — Grinding, tense, inevitable, foundational, dread.

**Concept:** This is a low, grinding drone that feels like the power source for a massive, decaying machine. It establishes the song's E Phrygian root but uses conflicting polyrhythms to create a foundation of immense, unresolved tension.

**Synthesis Approach:**
- **Engine / oscillator setup:** VCO 1 is the fundamental, tuned to a low E. Its subs provide a low octave and a perfect fifth above (1/8 and 1/3) for harmonic weight. VCO 2 is tuned slightly sharp to create a slow, menacing beat frequency against VCO 1.
- **Filter starting point:** The Moog Ladder filter is set low, with moderate resonance to add a low-mid growl without becoming a screech.
- **Envelope character:** The VCA envelope has a slow attack and very long decay/release, making it a continuous, breathing drone. The VCF envelope has an even slower attack, causing the filter to subtly open over 4-8 bars.
- **Modulation:** The core of the patch is sequencer 1 and 2 being clocked by two different, prime-numbered rhythm divisions (e.g., 5 and 7). This ensures their patterns phase against each other in a long, evolving, and unsettling cycle.
- **Effects:** Add a dark plate reverb with a long tail and a high-pass filter to keep the mud out. Follow with subtle saturation to bring out harmonics in the filter resonance.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
- **VCO 1 FREQ:** Tuned to E1 (approx. 9 o'clock on the knob)
- **VCO 1 SUB 1:** 1/8 (approx. 11 o'clock)
- **VCO 1 SUB 2:** 1/3 (approx. 2 o'clock)
- **VCO 2 FREQ:** Tuned to E1 + 4-5 cents (just past 9 o'clock)
- **RHYTHM GEN 1:** DIVIDE by 5 (approx. 12 o'clock)
- **RHYTHM GEN 2:** DIVIDE by 7 (approx. 2 o'clock)
- **VCF CUTOFF:** 9 o'clock (~150 Hz)
- **VCF RESONANCE:** 1 o'clock

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
- **When to reach for this sound:** Use this to open the track or as a textural bed during a sparse breakdown, letting the rhythmic tension build before the main drums crash in.
- **Digitakt integration hint:** Send only MIDI clock to channel 5. Use a MIDI LFO on the Digitakt to slowly modulate the VCF Cutoff (CC mapped in Ableton's "External Instrument" device) for an automated, evolving sweep.
- **One thing that makes it heavy or weird:** The heavy comes from the phase-beating of the detuned VCOs through the Moog filter. The weird is the rhythmic friction of the 5-against-7 sequencer timing, creating a pattern that feels like it's constantly on the verge of collapsing.

### Preset 2: Phrygian Swarm — Dissonant, metallic, urgent, chaotic, sharp.

**Concept:** A high-register, percussive sequence of metallic, bell-like tones. This sound is pure texture and chaos, using the Phrygian mode's characteristic flat-second to create a swarm of sharp, aggressive, and inharmonic notes.

**Synthesis Approach:**
- **Engine / oscillator setup:** VCO 1 is tuned to a high E. VCO 2 is tuned to F, the dissonant flat-second. The sub-oscillators are set to high, odd-numbered divisions (e.g., 1/11, 1/13) to generate clangorous, non-musical overtones that sound like detuned bells.
- **Filter starting point:** Cutoff is set higher, around the middle, with a touch of resonance to accentuate the metallic "ping."
- **Envelope character:** Both VCA and VCF envelopes are set to be extremely plucky: instant attack, very fast decay, and zero sustain. This transforms the drone instrument into a percussive one.
- **Modulation:** The two 4-step sequencers are programmed with a simple E Phrygian pattern (e.g., E, F, A, B) and clocked by fast, non-swinging rhythm generators. One sequencer is assigned to VCO 1 pitch, the other to VCO 2.
- **Effects:** A fast, rhythmic delay (dotted 8ths or 16ths) with lots of feedback will smear the notes into a chaotic texture. Add Ableton's Roar distortion after the delay to fuse it all together into a single wall of noise.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
- **VCO 1 FREQ:** Tuned to E4 (approx. 2 o'clock)
- **VCO 2 FREQ:** Tuned to F4 (approx. 2:30 o'clock)
- **SUB 1 / SUB 2 (both VCOs):** Set to high divisions like 1/11 and 1/13 (far left, between 7-8 o'clock)
- **VCF CUTOFF:** 1 o'clock (~1.5 kHz)
- **VCF RESONANCE:** 10 o'clock
- **VCF EG (DECAY):** 9 o'clock (very fast)
- **VCA EG (DECAY):** 9 o'clock (very fast)
- **RHYTHM GEN 1 & 2:** DIVIDE by 2 or 3 (fastest settings)

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
- **When to reach for this sound:** Layer this over a heavy, half-time groove. Let it serve the function of a hi-hat and a melodic element at the same time, providing frantic energy against a solid foundation.
- **Digitakt integration hint:** Send clock to channel 5. You can trigger the sequence on/off by sending a single MIDI note gate to the Subharmonicon to start it, then another to stop it.
- **One thing that makes it heavy or weird:** The inharmonic, clashing frequencies from the high sub-oscillator divisions create the weirdness. The heaviness comes from the sheer density and relentless speed of the sequence, especially when processed with delay and distortion.

## pigments

### Preset 1: Phrygian Rust Swarm — Grinding, unstable, tense, breathing, wide

**Concept:** A discordant, slowly evolving pad that feels like metal plates grinding against each other. It provides a bed of industrial texture that establishes the song's uneasy E Phrygian tonality.

**Synthesis Approach:**
