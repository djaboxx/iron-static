## Elektron Digitakt MK1

### Preset 1: Rusted Chain — Grinding, industrial, inevitable, broken, heavy.

**Concept:** This is a core industrial percussion kit built from metallic impacts and mechanical noise. It should sound less like a drum kit and more like a hostile machine coming to life in a decaying factory.

**Synthesis Approach:**
- **Samples:** Source short, sharp metallic sounds: a dropped wrench, a chain link drag, a failing HVAC unit kicking on. Resample them through heavy distortion and compression *before* loading them into the Digitakt to bake in the grit.
- **Filter:** Start with the multi-mode filter in low-pass mode on most tracks. Keep the cutoff relatively high (around 80-90) but use a touch of resonance (10-20) to accentuate the harsh transients.
- **Envelope:** Very fast attack on all sounds. For the "kick" (a low, distorted metallic chunk), use a short decay (30-40) and no sustain. For snares/hats (scrapes, clicks), use even shorter decays (10-20).
- **Modulation:** Assign LFO1 to sample start point on a hi-hat or percussion track. Use a random waveform, synced to 16th or 32nd notes, with a very low depth, to introduce subtle, inhuman variations.
- **Effects:** Use the master Overdrive (around 25-35) to glue the kit together. Send the "snare" hit aggressively to the Reverb with a short decay time to give it a sense of a small, reflective room.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
1.  **[SRC] Sample:** Load a distorted metal hit.
2.  **[AMP] Overdrive:** 40 (per track, for saturation)
3.  **[FILTER] Filter Frequency:** 85
4.  **[FILTER] Filter Resonance:** 20
5.  **[AMP] Hold:** 0
6.  **[AMP] Decay:** 25 (for a tight, punchy sound)
7.  **[LFO1] Destination:** Sample Start
8.  **[LFO1] Depth:** 2-5 (just enough for subtle movement)

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
- **When to reach for this sound:** This is a foundational rhythm bed for a high-energy, aggressive section. It provides texture and groove without occupying traditional kick/snare frequency space.
- **Digitakt integration hint:** Use Tracks 1-4 for the core pattern. Use parameter locks on **Filter Frequency** (CC74) to create rhythmic filter-sweeps on the hi-hat track, making the pattern breathe and evolve.
- **One thing that makes it heavy or weird:** The "kick" isn't a drum; it's a low-pitched, distorted impact sound, maybe a heavily processed 808 layered with a metal hit, tuned to the root note E. This gives the groove weight without sounding like a standard rock kit.

### Preset 2: Phrygian Ghost — Dissonant, melodic, haunting, detuned, digital.

**Concept:** This preset turns the Digitakt into a textural melodic instrument. It uses a single, longer sample sliced and tuned across the pads to create playable, ghostly phrases in the song's key.

**Synthesis Approach:**
- **Samples:** Source a single sample of something with harmonic content but no clear pitch, like radio static between stations, a granular synth texture, or even a short, processed vocal sample of VELA.
- **Slicing/Tuning:** Load the sample onto a single audio track. Use parameter locks on each step of a sequence to change the **sample start point** and **pitch**. Tune each step to a note in E Phrygian (E, F, G, A, B, C, D).
- **Filter:** Use the high-pass filter to thin out the sound, removing low-end mud and letting it float above the bass and drums. Set the cutoff low (20-30) and add a good amount of resonance (40-50) to create a whistling, ethereal tone.
- **Envelope:** Slower attack (10-15) to make the notes swell in. Set a long decay (80-100) and moderate sustain to let the notes ring out and overlap.
- **Effects:** Heavy use of the Delay and Reverb sends. Set the Delay to a dotted 8th note time and turn up the feedback to create evolving, washed-out tails.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
1.  **[SRC] Sample:** A 2-4 second textural audio file.
2.  **[SRC] Tune:** Use p-locks per step to create a melody.
3.  **[FILTER] Filter Type:** High Pass
4.  **[FILTER] Filter Frequency:** 30
5.  **[FILTER] Filter Resonance:** 45
6.  **[AMP] Attack:** 15
7.  **[LFO1] Destination:** Pan
8.  **[LFO1] Depth:** 64 (for wide, automatic stereo movement)

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
- **When to reach for this sound:** Use this as a lead line or a counter-melody during a sparse verse or bridge. It's perfect for creating tension and an unsettling atmosphere.
- **Digitakt integration hint:** Dedicate Audio Track 8 to this sound. Sequence a simple 16-step pattern, but use conditional trigs (e.g., 2:2, PRE, A:B) to make certain notes appear unpredictably, making the phrase feel less repetitive and more alive.
- **One thing that makes it heavy or weird:** The melody is created by sequencing different start points of a single chaotic sample and retuning them. This creates a disembodied, ghostly harmony where the timbre of each note is slightly different and unstable, fitting perfectly with the E Phrygian mode's dark character.

### Preset 1: Corroded Swarm — Abrasive, detuned, rhythmic, tense, wide

**Concept:** This is a distorted, detuned pad that replaces a traditional chord bed with a sense of industrial unease. It uses fast LFO modulation to create a rhythmic, almost granular texture that feels like a machine on the verge of breaking.

**Synthesis Approach:**
-   **Engine / oscillator setup:** Use both oscillators per voice. OSC 1 as a saw wave, OSC 2 as a saw+tri mix. Detune OSC 2 by +8-12 cents for a thick, phasing chorus. Add 2-voice unison for extra width and instability.
-   **Filter starting point:** Curtis 4-pole LP filter with cutoff around 60-70% open. Add a significant amount of resonance (around 75%) to create a strained, whistling overtone that bites.
-   **Envelope character:** Amp envelope with a fast attack (~10ms), full sustain, and a medium release (~400ms) to let notes bleed into each other. Filter envelope will be similar to let the LFO do the work.
-   **Modulation:** The core of the sound. LFO 1, set to a square or steep sawtooth wave, modulating the filter cutoff. Sync the LFO rate to a 16th or 32nd note subdivision of 108 BPM to create a gated, stuttering effect.
-   **Effects:** Use the built-in distortion effect, set to a moderate level to add grit and harmonic saturation without completely destroying the sound.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
1.  `osc_1_shape`: Sawtooth
2.  `osc_2_fine_tune`: +10 cents
3.  `filter_cutoff`: 800
4.  `filter_resonance`: 95
5.  `lfo_1_frequency`: Sync 1/16
6.  `lfo_1_shape`: Square
7.  `mod_slot_1_source`: LFO 1
8.  `mod_slot_1_destination`: Filter Cutoff

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
-   **When to reach for this sound:** Use it during tense build-ups or as the primary harmonic bed in a heavy chorus where a clean pad would sound too polite.
-   **Digitakt integration hint:** Sequence long, sustained chords from the Digitakt on MIDI channel 2. Use a Digitakt LFO to slowly automate the Rev2's `filter_cutoff` or the `lfo_1_frequency` to make the swarm evolve and become more frantic.
-   **One thing that makes it heavy or weird:** The high-resonance filter being rapidly modulated by a square-wave LFO creates a nasty, rhythmic tearing sound instead of a smooth filter sweep.

### Preset 2: Gridlock Pulse — Perverse, punchy, sub-heavy, focused, mean

**Concept:** This is a mono-mode unison bass patch designed for driving, staccato riffs. It has an incredibly sharp, percussive attack that cuts through the mix, followed by a thick, weighty body that carries the groove-metal influence.

**Synthesis Approach:**
-   **Engine / oscillator setup:** Set Unison to 4 voices for a tight, powerful mono sound. OSC 1 is a pulse wave with PWM (Pulse Width Modulation). OSC 2 is a sawtooth, one octave down, to add low-end weight.
-   **Filter starting point:** Curtis LP filter cutoff starts low (around 30%). Key is to use Envelope 3 (the dedicated filter envelope) with a high amount of modulation.
-   **Envelope character:** Both the Amp Env (VCA) and Filter Env (Env 3) should be very punchy: instant attack, very short decay (~150-200ms), and zero sustain. This creates the sharp, percussive "thump" that immediately gets out of the way.
-   **Modulation:** Use an LFO to slowly modulate the pulse width of OSC 1. This gives the sound a subtle, evolving texture so it doesn't feel static over a long riff.
-   **Effects:** A touch of distortion to add grit and aggression, helping it sit with heavy drums.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
1.  `unison_mode`: On (4 voices)
2.  `osc_1_shape`: Pulse
3.  `osc_2_octave`: -1
4.  `filter_env_amount`: +90
5.  `filter_env_decay`: 250
6.  `amp_env_decay`: 220
7.  `amp_env_sustain`: 0
8.  `mod_slot_1_source`: LFO 1
9.  `mod_slot_1_destination`: OSC 1 Shape (for PWM)

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
-   **When to reach for this sound:** The main riff. It's designed for 16th-note patterns that lock in with the kick drum, in the style of Lamb of God or Meshuggah.
-   **Digitakt integration hint:** Sequence this from the Digitakt on MIDI channel 2. Use parameter locks on the Digitakt's MIDI CC knobs to create filter cutoff variations per-step, adding movement and dynamics to the bassline.
-   **One thing that makes it heavy or weird:** The complete lack of sustain. The sound's weight comes from the initial, explosive attack of 4 unison voices hitting at once, which feels more like a physical impact than a sustained note.

**Synthesis Approach:**

-   **Filter starting point:** 4-pole resonant low-pass filter (Curtis LP). Start with Cutoff around 40%, Resonance at 60% to emphasize the harmonic sweep.

-   **Modulation:** Route the filter envelope to OSC2 Pitch. This will create the classic metallic sync sweep. LFO1 is a tempo-synced sawtooth wave, subtly modulating the filter cutoff for rhythmic interest.
-   **Effects:** A small amount of chorus to add stereo width.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
1.  **OSC2 Sync:** On

3.  **Filter Cutoff (CC 33):** ~550
4.  **Filter Resonance (CC 34):** ~75
5.  **Filter Env Amount:** +80
6.  **Filter Env Decay:** ~400
7.  **LFO1 Freq (CC 75):** Set to 1/8th note sync
8.  **FX Depth/Mix (CC 18):** ~25 (Chorus)

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
-   **When to reach for this sound:** Use for off-beat chord stabs during a verse or a build-up section to create tension and a sense of mechanical movement.
-   **Digitakt integration hint:** Sequence from Digitakt MIDI track 4 (ch4). Use parameter locks on **CC33 (Filter Cutoff)** to create dynamic, evolving patterns. A slight random LFO on the Digitakt track targeting cutoff can add extra life.

- **Effects:** The Brute Factor is a core part of the sound generation circuit here, pushed to 75% or higher to mangle the feedback loop into complex noise.
