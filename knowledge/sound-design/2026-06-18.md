## Elektron Digitakt MK1

### Preset 1: Corroded Foundation — Crushing, distorted, industrial, grid, weight

**Concept:** This is a core drum kit built from overdriven, bit-crushed samples of metal and machinery. It provides a heavy, syncopated industrial groove that feels like a massive, rusted engine turning over.

**Synthesis Approach:**
- **Sample Source & Manipulation:** Load short, percussive samples of metal impacts, factory sounds, and a heavily saturated 808-style kick. Use the Digitakt's sample start/end trimming to find the most aggressive transients. Apply a high amount of overdrive to the kick and snare samples.
- **Filter starting point:** Keep the filter wide open on most tracks, but use the low-pass filter on the kick (Track 1) with a slight resonance bump to add low-end pressure.
- **Envelope character:** AMP envelopes should be tight and plucky. Instant attack, short decay (30-50ms), zero sustain, and minimal release for maximum punch.
- **Modulation:** Use the LFO assigned to the sample's pitch on the hi-hat track (e.g., Track 3) with a very low depth and a random waveform to create subtle, human-like inconsistencies.
- **Effects:** Use the master Overdrive effect at around 35-45%. Send the snare/clap track heavily to a short, dark Reverb to give it a sense of space without washing it out.

**Parameter Starting Points:**
1.  **[TRK 1: Kick]**: `SMP` (Sample): A kick with a long, distorted tail.
2.  **[TRK 1: Kick]**: `DRV` (Overdrive): 75
3.  **[TRK 2: Snare]**: `SMP`: A sample of a metal pipe being struck.
4.  **[TRK 2: Snare]**: `BIT` (Bit Reduction): 24 (p-lock higher values on occasional hits for grit)
5.  **[FLTR]**: `FREQ` (Filter Frequency): 40 (on kick track only)
6.  **[FLTR]**: `RES` (Filter Resonance): 68 (on kick track only)
7.  **[AMP]**: `DEC` (Decay): 40 on most tracks
8.  **[FX]**: Master Overdrive: 40

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
- **When to reach for this sound:** The primary rhythmic backbone for heavy, driving sections. It's built to carry the weight of distorted bass and aggressive synth lines.
- **Digitakt integration hint:** Use audio tracks 1-4 for this kit. Parameter-lock the `BIT` reduction and `Filter Frequency` (CC74) on snare and hat tracks to create variations and fills without changing the core pattern.
- **One thing that makes it heavy or weird:** The combination of heavy sample-level overdrive and master overdrive glues the kit together with a thick layer of saturation, making it feel massive and unified.

### Preset 2: Unstable Chatter — Glitching, polyrhythmic, textural, off-kilter

**Concept:** A textural loop made from a non-musical sample, sequenced with conditional trigs to create a skittering, unstable rhythmic bed. This isn't a drum beat, but a layer of chaotic machine noise that has been forced into a groove.

**Synthesis Approach:**

- **Modulation:** Assign LFO1 to `Filter Frequency` (CC74) with a slow, random wave. This will make the texture evolve and shift over time, never repeating exactly.
- **Effects:** Heavy send to the Delay, with high feedback and the filter set to darken the repeats, creating a murky, trailing wash of sound.

**Parameter Starting Points:**

2.  **[SRC]**: `LEN` (Sample Length): 0.50 (very short)
3.  **[FLTR]**: `TYPE`: BP (Band Pass)
4.  **[FLTR]**: `FREQ` (Filter Frequency): 80
5.  **[FLTR]**: `RES` (Filter Resonance): 70
6.  **[LFO1]**: `DEST`: Filter Frequency
7.  **[LFO1]**: `SPD` (Speed): 16
8.  **[LFO1]**: `DEP` (Depth/`LFO1 Depth` CC109): 45

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
- **When to reach for this sound:** Use as a top layer during intros, breakdowns, or behind a sparser beat to create tension and a sense of unease. It can function as a hi-hat pattern that's about to fall apart.

### Preset 1: Rust Grinder — Grinding, tense, evolving, metallic drone

**Concept:** This is a low, abrasive pad designed to provide harmonic tension and a constant sense of industrial dread. It should feel like a massive, poorly-maintained machine breathing in time with the track.

**Synthesis Approach:**
-   **Engine / oscillator setup:** Engine 1: Wavetable, `Building_Materials/Grinder.wav` table, 4 voices of unison, 15% detune. Engine 2: Analog, Saw wave, coarse-tuned -12 semitones, 2 voices unison.
-   **Filter starting point:** Filter 1: Low-pass 24dB/oct (`SEM` mode), cutoff around 400Hz, resonance at 60%. Filter 2: Comb filter, modest feedback, mixed in parallel at 30%.
-   **Envelope character:** Amp Env: Slow attack (300-500ms), full sustain, medium release (400ms). Filter Env: similar but with a slightly shorter attack to let the initial grind through.
-   **Modulation:** LFO1 synced to 1/4 note, triangle wave, modulating Wavetable Position (depth: 0.20) and F1 Cutoff (depth: 0.10). Assign Macro 1 to LFO1 Rate and F1 Resonance.
-   **Effects:** Distortion (Tape saturation, 4dB drive), followed by a short Reverb (Plate, 1.5s decay, 25% wet).

**Parameter Starting Points:**
-   `Engine 1 Wavetable`: Grinder.wav
-   `Engine 1 Unison Detune`: 15%
-   `F1_CUTOFF`: 400 Hz
-   `F1_RES`: 0.60
-   `LFO 1 Rate`: 0.25 Hz (1/4 note @ 108 BPM)
-   `Macro 1 (CC20)`: Assigned to LFO1 Rate & F1_RES
-   `FX A Drive`: 4.0 dB
-   `FX B Wet/Dry`: 25%

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
-   When to reach for this sound: Underpinning a heavy verse or a breakdown section to provide a layer of filth and texture that moves without being a distinct melodic part.
-   Digitakt integration hint: Sequence long, sustained notes on MIDI Channel 8. Use the Digitakt's MIDI LFO to slowly sweep Macro 1 (CC20) over 16-32 bars to create a long, evolving performance.

### Preset 2: Phrygian Glitch — Shattered, chromatic, percussive, urgent

**Concept:** A sharp, aggressive, and unpredictable percussive sequence. It's designed for chaotic, fast-moving lines that cut through the mix and add a sense of digital instability and panic.

**Synthesis Approach:**
-   **Engine / oscillator setup:** Engine 1: Harmonic. Set Ratio to 1.500 (a dissonant fifth), and modulate the partials with a random source. Engine 2: Off.
-   **Filter starting point:** Filter 1: High-pass 12dB/oct (`MS-20` mode), cutoff at 800Hz, a touch of resonance to add a sharp peak.
-   **Envelope character:** Amp Env: Instant attack, 50ms decay, zero sustain, 20ms release. Very plucky and short.
-   **Modulation:** Use the internal Arpeggiator/Sequencer. Set to 16th notes, Random pattern, 2-octave range. Route the Sequencer's velocity output to the Harmonic Engine's "Partials Amp" parameter to make some notes pop out unpredictably.
-   **Effects:** Bit Crusher (8-bit depth), followed by a fast Ping-Pong Delay (synced dotted 16th notes, 30% feedback).

**Parameter Starting Points:**
-   `Engine 1 Type`: Harmonic
-   `Harmonic Ratio`: 1.500
-   `Amp Env Decay`: 50 ms
-   `Amp Env Sustain`: 0.0
-   `F1_CUTOFF`: 800 Hz
-   `Arp Rate`: 1/16
-   `Arp Pattern`: Random
-   `FX A Bit Depth`: 8-bit

**IRON STATIC Usage:**
-   When to reach for this sound: As an intermittent textural layer during a chorus or transition, or as the main hook for a frenetic bridge section. Hold down a single E note and let the arp do the work.
-   Digitakt integration hint: Send a simple pattern of 8th or 4th notes on MIDI Ch. 8 to trigger the arp. Automate Macro 2 (CC21), assigned to the Arp Gate length and Delay Feedback, to create builds and breakdowns in the pattern's intensity.
-   One thing that makes it heavy or weird: The combination of the inharmonic Harmonic engine and the random arpeggiator creates melodies that intentionally fight against the E Phrygian scale, providing purposeful dissonance and chaos.
